Garlic is often planted in autumn. Over winter it builds roots, then grows quickly in spring and gives larger bulbs by early summer. Here is how to plant garlic in autumn and what to keep in mind.

## When to plant

The aim is for garlic to root before the frosts without growing much above ground. As a general rule, plant about 3–4 weeks before the ground freezes. In most of Georgia that means late October to early November. In the mountains it is earlier, sometimes late September, and in warmer regions slightly later. The exact timing depends on your local weather.

## Which type to choose

- **Hardneck garlic:** copes better with cold winters, sends up a flower stalk (scape) in early summer, and has large cloves. It stores for a somewhat shorter time.
- **Softneck garlic:** better suited to warmer regions and stores for a long time.
- **Winter and spring garlic:** winter garlic is planted in autumn, spring garlic in spring.
- Start with healthy, local or quality seed garlic rather than market garlic of unknown origin, which may carry disease.

## Preparing the site and soil

- Choose a sunny spot with good drainage. Garlic does not like waterlogged soil.
- The soil should be loose and fertile. Slightly acidic to neutral soil (pH about 6–7) is generally good. [See soil pH here](/en/blog/soil-ph/).
- Before planting, dig the soil over and, if needed, add well-rotted compost or organic fertilizer. Do not use fresh manure.
- Do not plant garlic in the same spot, or after other alliums (onion, garlic), year after year. A 3–4 year rotation reduces disease build-up.

## How to plant

1. Break the bulb into cloves a day or two before planting. Plant healthy, large cloves.
2. Plant each clove **pointed end up**.
3. Plant about 4–6 cm deep.
4. Leave about 10–15 cm between cloves and 25–30 cm between rows.
5. Lightly level the soil afterwards and water if it is dry.

## Mulching for winter

When the frosts begin, cover the bed with about 5–10 cm of mulch (straw, dry leaves, compost). This protects the soil from sharp swings in frost and moisture. In spring, as the weather warms, remove any excess mulch.

## Spring care and harvest

- In spring, when garlic starts growing again, feeding with nitrogen works well. Check the product label for the dose.
- In a dry spring, water moderately. Too much moisture encourages rot.
- Remove weeds, since garlic cannot compete with them.
- Cut the scapes of hardneck types so the plant puts its energy into bulb size.
- Harvest is usually in early to mid summer, when about a third to a half of the lower leaves have turned yellow and dried. Stop watering 2–3 weeks before harvest.
- Cure the harvested garlic in a shaded, well-ventilated place for 2–4 weeks, then store it.
